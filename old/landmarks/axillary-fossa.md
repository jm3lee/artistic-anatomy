## Summary

The **axillary fossa** is the hollow between the
{{ link('anterior-axillary-fold') }}
and the
{{ link('posterior-axillary-fold') }}.
It opens toward the arm, yet its apex tucks deep toward the
{{ link('clavicle') }}.
Here nerves and vessels dive from the neck into the limb.
A thin sheet of axillary fascia spans the hollow like a hammock, softening the
transition from chest to arm while trapping the lymph nodes and fat that fill
the armpit.

Raise the arm and the fossa stretches into a taut pyramid.
Its walls are clearly divided by the two folds and the rib cage lined with the
{{ link('serratus-anterior') }}.
Let the arm relax and the hollow collapses, creating subtle planes, shadows, and
often a tuft of hair that helps orient the viewer.
Use the shape of the fossa to show how the torso narrows into the upper limb and
to suggest the swing of the scapula as it glides on the ribs.
