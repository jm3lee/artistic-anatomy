**Which {{trc}} head forms the lateral bulge when you extend your arm?**

a. Medial head
a. Anconeus
a. Lateral head
a. Long head

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
c

The **lateral head** of the triceps brachii is what creates the obvious “bulge” on the outer (lateral) side of your arm when you straighten (extend) your elbow.

* **Location & Origin**
  * Arises from the posterior surface of the humerus, above the radial groove, on the lateral aspect.

* **Why it bulges**
  * Its fibers lie closest to the skin on the outside of the arm, so when they contract—pulling the forearm straight—they push outward against the overlying fascia.

* **In contrast**
  * The **long head** runs more medially and attaches to the scapula, so it contributes more to overall arm mass and shoulder stability.
  * The **medial head** sits deepest, largely covered by the other two heads.
  * The **anconeus** is a small accessory muscle at the elbow, not visible as a bulge.

Thus, when you extend your arm, it’s the lateral head you see most prominently on the outer curve of the triceps.
