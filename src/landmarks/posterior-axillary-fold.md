## Summary

The **posterior axillary fold** is the back wall of the armpit where the
{{ link('ld') }} blends with the {{ link('teres-major') }}. These muscles sweep
from the {{ link('scapula') }} onto the {{ link('humerus') }}. They form a firm ridge
that runs from the inferior angle of the scapula toward the arm. The fold defines
the rear border of the axilla and separates the trunk from the upper limb.

Ask a model to extend or internally rotate the shoulder and the fold tightens
into a crisp strap that casts a clear shadow across the rib cage. In relaxed
poses it softens into a gentle roll, but the fold still hints at the cylindrical
latissimus mass and the smaller teres major nestled along its lower edge. Use
that contrast in volume to stage the shoulder blade and to show how the back
tapers into the arm.
