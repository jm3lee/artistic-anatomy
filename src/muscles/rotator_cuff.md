## Function

Stabilizes the shoulder joint.

Rotates and elevates the arm.

## Origins

All four rotator‑cuff muscles originate on the scapula:

* **Supraspinatus**
  * Originates from the supraspinous fossa of the scapula
* **Infraspinatus**
  * Originates from the infraspinous fossa of the scapula
* **Teres minor**
  * Originates from the lateral border of the scapula
* **Subscapularis**
  * Originates from the subscapular fossa (anterior surface) of the scapula

Each muscle spans from the scapula to attach at the proximal (upper) end of the {{humerus|linkicon}}—the long bone of the upper arm—forming the musculotendinous “cuff” that stabilizes the glenohumeral (shoulder) joint.

## Insertions

Of the four rotator‐cuff muscles, only **subscapularis** inserts on the anterior surface of the {{humerus|linkicon}}:

* **Subscapularis**
  * **Insertion:** Lesser tubercle of the {{humerus|linkicon}} (anterior aspect)
  * **Action:** Medially rotates and adducts the {{humerus|linkicon}}

The other three insert on the greater tubercle, which lies more posterior‑lateral:

* **Supraspinatus**
  * **Insertion:** Superior facet of the greater tubercle
  * **Action:** Abducts the {{humerus|linkicon}}
* **Infraspinatus**
  * **Insertion:** Middle facet of the greater tubercle
  * **Action:** Laterally rotates the {{humerus|linkicon}}
* **Teres minor**
  * **Insertion:** Inferior facet of the greater tubercle
  * **Action:** Laterally rotates and assists in adduction of the
    {{humerus|linkicon}}
