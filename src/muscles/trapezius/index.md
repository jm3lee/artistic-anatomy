## Anatomy

The trapezius drapes over the upper back like a kite-shaped cloak. Its broad
origin spans the external occipital protuberance, the nuchal ligament, and the
spinous processes down to T12. From that tendinous sheet the fibers narrow to
three distinct bands that seize the lateral {{ linktitle('clavicle') }}, the
{{ linktitle('acromion-process') }}, and the spine of the
{{ linktitle('scapula') }}. Because the muscle attaches to bone along the
shoulder girdle rather than the humerus, every contraction adjusts how the
scapula glides across the rib cage, indirectly steering the arm.

### Fiber regions

- **Upper fibers** elevate the shoulder and subtly extend the neck. They form
  the contour rising from the {{ linktitle('scm') }} toward the
  acromion.
- **Middle fibers** sweep horizontally to retract the scapula, partnering with
  {{ linktitle('rhomboid-major') }} and {{ linktitle('rhmin') }} to
  draw the shoulder blades toward the spine.
- **Lower fibers** anchor on the lower thoracic spines and pull the scapula
  downward while rotating the glenoid upward. Their line of action
  collaborates with {{ linktitle('sa') }} during overhead
  reaches.

### Coordinated actions

The trapezius rarely works alone. During elevation of the arm, the upper and
lower fibers form a force couple that stabilizes the scapula for
{{ linktitle('dt') }} and {{ linktitle('supraspinatus') }}. When carrying
weight, the middle fibers brace against lateral drift while the lower fibers
prevent shrugging. Remember to depict the scapular spine sliding as a single
lever: rotation around the chest happens through the trapezius tugging from
multiple directions rather than a hinge at one point.

## Drawing strategy

### Surface landmarks

- The superior nuchal line defines the cranial base of the trapezius. Indicate
  a soft ridge instead of a hard groove.
- Follow the scapular spine to the acromion; that ridge maps the mid-fiber
  insertion and points to the peak of the shoulder mass.
- The lower fibers converge into a narrow tendon that forms a shallow diamond
  above the T12 landmark. Suggest it with tonal shifts rather than deep
  creases.

### Gesture and rhythm

Lay in the outer kite silhouette first, then carve the flow of fibers. The
upper band wraps from the neck to the shoulder in a broad "C" curve, the middle
band runs level across the thorax, and the lower band descends in a sharp "V"
toward the spine. Light anatomical cues—such as the contrast between
{{ linktitle('ld') }} volume and trapezius plane—clarify depth
without over-modeling.

## Practice prompts

1. Sketch three quick poses showing scapular elevation, depression, and upward
   rotation. Emphasize how the trapezius shifts the shoulder blade position.
2. Layer the trapezius over a simplified rib cage and {{ linktitle('spine') }}.
   Trace the insertions on the clavicle and scapular spine before adding tonal
   modeling.
3. Compare the trapezius to the levator scapulae in a neck study. Note where the
   forms separate near the superior angle of the scapula.
